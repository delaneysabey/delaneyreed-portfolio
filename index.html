Reel breakdown

A closer look at the systems and decisions behind the work.

01 Ed Rig

Student Accomplice | 0:00-0:09

Main character rig for a BYU senior capstone short film. I helped create and maintain the rig for Ed, one of the film's three main characters. Throughout production, I collaborated closely with animators to refine the rig for intuitive controls, animator-friendly workflows, and strong deformations based on shot feedback.

I also rigged several character props, including the trash can Ed falls into and his clipboard and pen, ensuring they could be easily controlled and integrated into his performance.

Tools / Software: Maya, Python. Parts of the rigging workflow were automated using Python tools I developed for Maya.

02 Stretchy Suspender Rig

Student Accomplice | 0:09-0:10

Custom stretchy suspender rig developed for Student Accomplice. I designed and implemented a custom rig for Ed's suspenders, which needed to stretch dramatically during a car-chase sequence while remaining highly art-directable for animators.

The final system uses primary Bezier controls to define the overall position and curvature of the suspenders, with additional secondary controls that follow the primary controls while allowing finer shape adjustments.

The system was developed iteratively with the animation team. Based on animator feedback and shot requirements, I refined the controls and behavior to balance ease of use with the flexibility needed to achieve the exaggerated shapes required by the sequence.

Tools / Software: Maya.

03 Arm Autorigger

Arm autorigging tool | 0:10-0:14

As part of a three-person rigging team, I helped develop a modular autorigger by taking ownership of the arm and hand system. Given an existing arm and hand joint chain, my Python tool automatically builds the necessary rig controls and supporting systems.

The resulting rig includes IK/FK switching for the arm and independent IK/FK switching for each finger, allowing animators to use any combination of IK and FK across the arm and fingers. Developing the system procedurally also made iteration faster: when changes were requested, I could update the script and rebuild the rig rather than recreating it manually.

Tools / Software: Python, Maya.

04 Nolan & Nelson

Personal project | 0:15-0:25

Nolan & Nelson is an ongoing solo animated web series created entirely in Blender. I handle the full 3D production pipeline: sculpting and modeling the two dinosaur characters, building and rigging the characters, creating environments, animating shots, and editing the final videos. I also developed a procedural grass tool to streamline environment creation.

I began the project with very little Blender experience and learned the software while producing the first short. The project has expanded my experience across the 3D pipeline, helped me explore new creative and technical workflows, and made me comfortable working in a new software. As the series continues, I plan to create additional episodes and develop more tools to automate my one-person production workflow.

Tools / Software: Blender, Python.

05 Ninja Rig

Shrineflow | 0:25-0:34

I created and maintained the rig for the Ninja character in Shrineflow, a BYU senior capstone video game project. For this rig, I reused and adapted rigging systems that my team and I had previously developed for Student Accomplice, applying existing tools and workflows to a new character and a different type of production.

Tools / Software: Maya.

06 Crab Pot

BYU animation coursework assignment | 0:34-0:39

A mimic character created for a BYU animation assignment, in which an ordinary kitchen pot transforms to reveal itself as a crab-like creature. I created the character from modeling through final animation, including modeling, rigging, and animating the transforming crab pot. I also modeled the surrounding environment and textured the character and environment assets using Substance Painter.

For the final shot, I created a cloth simulation in Maya for the curtains blowing in the background and rendered the scene using Arnold.

Tools / Software: Maya, Substance Painter, Arnold.

07 Still Life

BYU animation coursework assignment | 0:39-0:43

A 3D still life featuring an unfinished wooden clock surrounded by tools on a workbench. I created the scene from modeling through final render, using the environment to tell a story and make the clock feel actively in the process of being carved and assembled.

I modeled the scene in Maya and textured the assets in Substance Painter. For the work gloves, I used Houdini to create a cloth simulation with natural wrinkles and folds. I developed the carved and engraved details of the unfinished wood through texturing in Substance Painter before lighting and rendering the final scene with Arnold in Maya.

Tools / Software: Maya, Substance Painter, Houdini, Arnold.

08 Custom MetaHuman Clothing

Unreal Engine ancient Jerusalem recreation | 0:43-0:50

As a student employee for the BYU Religion Department, I contributed to a virtual recreation of ancient Jerusalem during the time of Christ. My role included creating MetaHuman characters and developing custom clothing for them.

I created the garments in Marvelous Designer and textured them using Substance Painter and Substance Designer. I used Substance Designer to develop custom striped patterns that could be varied across different garments and characters. I then brought the clothing into Maya, bound it to the MetaHuman skeleton, and refined the skin weights to minimize clipping and unwanted deformations during movement. Finally, I integrated the finished clothing and characters into Unreal Engine for use in the virtual environment.

Tools / Software: Unreal Engine, Marvelous Designer, Maya, Substance Painter, Substance Designer.

09 Midas Cinematic - DNEG Internship

0:50-0:59

During my internship at DNEG in London, I worked with a team of four other interns to create a cinematic in Unreal Engine. With access to DNEG's motion capture studio and mentorship from DNEG artists, we recorded motion capture performances and retargeted the animation onto MetaHuman characters.

Our cinematic retold the story of King Midas, following Midas as he discovers his ability to turn objects to gold before accidentally turning his daughter to gold.

My primary contribution was creating Midas's custom clothing in Marvelous Designer and skinning it to the character in Maya, building on the clothing workflow I had previously developed for the Jerusalem MetaHuman project. Through the project, I also gained hands-on experience with motion capture, retargeting, Unreal Engine, and virtual production workflows.

Tools: Unreal Engine, Marvelous Designer, Maya, Substance Painter.
